Bellevue College · Accessible Course Materials

PDF Decision Tree

This guide helps you choose the best way to share and maintain accessible course materials. When possible, use accessible web pages and document formats rather than PDFs — they are far easier to make accessible and to keep current.

Have an inaccessible PDF and aren't sure what to do next? Start with Question 1 and follow the route that fits.

Question 2 · Ownership

Do you know who owns and/or created the PDF or its content?

YesNo
YesContinue to the copyright question.
Question 3		 No — ResultTreat the document as third-party content until its source is confirmed. Without knowing who created or owns it, you cannot determine whether you have the right to remediate, post, or adapt it. Check the file's document properties and any headers, footers, or cover pages for a publisher, author, or institution. If the source is still unclear, contact the BC Library Media Center for help identifying the original work and locating an accessible, properly licensed version you can link to instead. Interactive eLearning can help once the source is known.

Do not remediate or redistribute a document of unknown origin. Linking to a verified published version is almost always the faster and safer path.

Question 3c · Copyright

Copyright · Is the document in the public domain or covered by fair use?

YesNo / Not sure
Yes — ResultIf the document is in the public domain or your use meets the fair use guidelines, you may proceed. Continue.
Question 4 		No / Not sure — ResultYou will need to obtain copyright permission before using or remediating the document. Follow the steps in Getting Copyright Permission — this generally means identifying the rights holder (often the publisher rather than the author), submitting a written request that describes exactly how the material will be used, and keeping a record of the permission you receive. Once permission is granted, you can proceed with remediation.

Note: If you're unsure whether you need permission or how to request it, contact Interactive eLearning or the BC Library Media Center.

Question 7

Is there an equivalent web resource available?

YesNo
Yes — ResultLink to the web resource using a persistent link within your course. Your content may be available free online, through the BC Library Media Center databases, or from a publisher (ePub or eBook version). Always use descriptive, accessible link text.

Instead of a bare URL like https://youtu.be/5MuIMqhT8DM, write: VIDEO: Watch "Sleep Is Your Superpower" (TED Talk by Matt Walker).

Form · Did you or someone internally at BC create this form?

YesNo
Yes — ResultRecreate the form in an accessible format using a college-approved tool — Qualtrics or Microsoft Forms.

If a paper form must be kept as is, contact the Disability Resource Center (DRC) for support from a remediation specialist, and provide an accommodation for any individual who needs one.

 No — ResultIf this is an external form owned by a federal or state government agency (e.g., HR employment files, tax forms, legal documents), keep the PDF as is. You may need to provide an accommodation if the required form is not accessible to an individual.

Question 9

Does the PDF include visual design elements that make it difficult to remediate (complex graphs or charts, an infographic, a flyer, or a file created in another tool such as Adobe InDesign)?

YesNo
Yes — ResultWhere the file was created in a design tool (Adobe InDesign, Adobe Express, or similar), use that tool's built-in accessibility features before exporting — it greatly reduces the remediation work needed later in Adobe Acrobat. No — ResultThe PDF can be remediated directly. Use Adobe Acrobat Pro to tag and check the document, or contact Interactive eLearning for support.

Where to get help

This decision tree doesn't cover every scenario. For anything not addressed here — or for help with redesigning, remediating, or finding accessible alternatives — contact Interactive eLearning.

  • Interactive eLearning — redesigning content, remediation support, general questions
  • BC Library Media Center — finding accessible versions, persistent links, database resources
  • Disability Resource Center (DRC) — accommodations and form remediation specialists
